# Proposed baseline bequest design: pooled wealth at the last adult's death

**Status (7 October 2026):** The pooled non-pension bequest rule is implemented and always active for both natural deaths (`Death`) and deaths simulated by population alignment (`DeathAlignment`). Emigration (`EmigrationAlignment`) does not trigger bequests. The two ageing scenarios must be rerun: their most recent archived outputs excluded alignment deaths. A separate `transferDcPensionAtDeath` switch controls whether a remaining modelled pension pot is transferred at either type of death before retirement. Both ageing-scenario YAML files set it to `true`. The legacy 2021 input needs no pension-component split under the restored modelled-pot convention. This is the sole non-pension inheritance method. The previous first-death mechanism is documented for historical reference in [wealth-inheritance-first-death-alternative.md](wealth-inheritance-first-death-alternative.md) and is no longer selectable. Matteo's original outline and the user's refinements are recorded in [wealth-inheritance-matteo-proposal.md](wealth-inheritance-matteo-proposal.md).

## Purpose and accounting unit

This design models a benefit unit's **pooled non-pension wealth**. A first death in a couple leaves the unit's financial balance and home with the surviving partner. The survivor has full control: their consumption and later investment can change the amount left. Children of the first deceased partner remain **eligible to share the wealth remaining at the final death**, including if the survivor later re-partners. No child has a fixed, protected first-death claim under this rule.

The trigger is the **death of the last responsible adult** in the benefit unit, not literally the last person: dependent children may remain. Settle deaths after mortality and population alignment and before the model removes units without a responsible adult. Natural and alignment deaths receive the same inheritance treatment. If both partners die in one year, including a mixture of the two death causes, settle their pooled non-pension wealth once. A divorce or separation follows the existing wealth-division process; it is not itself a bequest.

**Pooled estate reference:** Use a deceased responsible adult, identified by the benefit unit's male/female responsible-adult roles, for synthetic-recipient matching and ledger attribution. Never use a dependent child. If both responsible adults die in the same annual period, select the higher-ID adult as a deterministic convention; the annual model does not establish within-year death order. Each person's pension transfer still uses its actual owner, and the death-event ledger records all deaths separately.

The unweighted population-alignment algorithm first attempts domestic migration. For surplus age cells below 65, it then attempts whole-benefit-unit emigration; remaining surplus people can be removed as simulated deaths at any age, subject to its family safeguards. Shortfalls are filled by immigrant clones. This inheritance revision respects those existing exit classifications; it does not change population targets, mortality probabilities or the alignment algorithm. Holding age shares fixed alone does not identify a unique change in longevity: births and migration also affect the age structure.

At an alignment death with a surviving partner, the unit retains its pooled non-pension wealth, the survivor's personal balance is updated, and the deceased's child branches remain eligible for the final estate. Any eligible unaccessed pension pot transfers under the partner-first rule. If no responsible adult survives and all responsible-adult exits are deaths, the pooled financial and housing estate is distributed rather than recorded as wealth leaving through alignment. An alignment-deceased child is represented by their living descendants in the same way as a naturally deceased child.

Emigrants leave with wealth and generate no bequest. Where mortality is followed by emigration of the remaining partner before annual settlement, the pooled non-pension balance leaves with that unit. The natural decedent's eligible pension pot is recorded as leaving with the emigrating partner who was alive during the mortality stage, rather than paid to children. Emigrant pension stock, final-unit non-pension wealth, and alignment-created entrants are recorded in `alignment_wealth_flows.csv`. Death estates transferred within the population are **not also recorded as external alignment outflows**. Its `partner_retained_bookkeeping` rows are personal-balance adjustments, **not aggregate inflows or outflows**; unit and person rows describe different asset components. Existing minor-trust exit treatment remains separately visible in the transfer ledger; this revision does not add a new inheritance rule for a deceased minor's trust.

## Non-pension estate and recipients

At the final death, form one estate from the unit's net financial balance **after unsecured debt** plus **net housing equity after mortgage debt**. Use `max(0, net amount)` as the distributable estate; never pass a negative balance to heirs. Record any debt shortfall or write-off separately. Treat any remaining home as sold at this point and distribute its net equity as financial cash in the one-off capital windfall. The transfer is not annual income or current disposable income. It enters an adult recipient's personal financial balance and their benefit unit's non-housing, non-pension balance exactly once; it then affects future investment returns and consumption through the ordinary wealth process.

First try all **distinct, linked child branches** of the final deceased adult and of earlier deceased partners whose pooled wealth passed to that adult. Preserve these links in a personal lineage register when a partner dies, carry them through a later union or move, and deduplicate common children. If a child has already died, their living descendants represent that branch where reliable links exist. When a survivor joins a new partner, both adults' carried lineages join the recipient pool for whatever wealth remains at that new unit's final death; if one of them subsequently dies, their carried lineages pass onward with the pooled wealth. **All recorded child branches, including those carried from earlier deceased partners and those of the new couple, share equally** in the final pooled estate. This is a deliberately simple pooling convention: it preserves *eligibility* for the first partner's children but does not preserve that partner's initial wealth amount or give their children a protected percentage.

For a child or synthetic recipient under the model's age of responsibility, keep the receipt in a personal trust balance until adulthood. It is reported as that person's wealth but does not become a guardian's spendable cash. A dependent child left in the deceased unit without a responsible adult may be removed by the model's cleanup; place that child in a valid receiving unit before retaining a trust, or record the transfer as out of sample. Update both personal and benefit-unit balances for adult recipients so moves and later partnerships do not lose or duplicate the transfer. Even though the first death creates no child payment, update the survivor's personal wealth state consistently with their newly owned unit balance before they can form a new unit.

### No identified child: synthetic beneficiaries

When **no linked or recorded unresolved child branch is available**, draw up to `n` distinct, living simulated beneficiaries. Set **`n = 2` for the baseline**, keep it configurable, and use `n = 3` as an initial sensitivity case. These are **synthetic recipients**, not verified relatives. The match uses the last surviving adult's age, region, and education. For each recipient draw an integer age gap uniformly from 20 through 30 years; first seek a person of exactly that age, in the same region, with the same completed education category. Exclude the deceased and select different benefit units where possible.

If no candidate is available, try an adjacent completed education category (`Low`–`Medium`–`High`) in the same region and age; then any completed education in that cell; then relax age by one year at a time while retaining the region. Treat `InEducation` separately rather than as an adjacent completed level. Any further national fallback should be logged as a distinct tier so its impact can be assessed. Never silently select a dead person, duplicate a recipient, or invent a recipient outside the simulated population. Reserve one equal estate share per intended beneficiary; if fewer than `n` eligible recipients can be found even after relaxation, pay the matched shares and record the remainder as unmatched.

The random selection must be reproducible from the run seed and estate identity and must not perturb the model's unrelated random draws. Log the matched age gap, region/education match tier, intended and actual `n`, recipient IDs, and transferred values. These assumptions can mechanically affect age and education wealth inequality; compare `n = 2` and `n = 3`, and report how much estate value is assigned synthetically.

If at least one linked child exists but another child is known from a source record yet cannot be linked to a simulated person, preserve that **known unresolved child branch** and assign its equal share to **one matched synthetic recipient**. Never silently increase the linked children's shares. Record the original branch and its synthetic stand-in in the transfer ledger. Truly unrecorded children cannot be detected from the current 2021 initial data, so this limitation remains. The Wave 15 `nchildlv` variable is outside the current design and is not required for a 2021 start.

**Shared-child identity:** Before an estate's equal shares are calculated, combine the parents' eligible branches and remove any unresolved source-child entry already represented by a linked child in that combined list. Two records of the same child create one entitlement. Two distinct simulated people remain distinct even if both originated from the same source record; do not collapse cloned people merely by source ID. Genuinely different unresolved children retain their reserved shares. This pooled-list correction was implemented on 7 October 2026 and is covered by regression tests. The responsible-adult reference correction is also implemented; both changes are recorded in the [code walkthrough](wealth-inheritance-code-walkthrough.md).

## Pension pots: separate from pooled non-pension wealth

Pension wealth remains **individual** after the benefit-unit starting total is divided equally between partners. SimPaths treats that total as a single **modelled pot** for each person who has not retired. The pot earns returns and receives new contributions until modelled retirement; retirement converts it into a lump sum and pension income and sets the pension wealth stock to zero. An initially retired person's starting stock is also cleared at the first annual pension update, while their recorded pension income continues. When `transferDcPensionAtDeath: true`, transfer a remaining pot only if its owner dies before modelled retirement, even if a partner survives. When `false`, remove the deceased person's pension stock without a pension bequest; housing and financial bequests still occur under the pooled rule. A transfer is a one-off financial receipt, not continued pension income. Do not include the pot in the last-survivor non-pension estate or distribute it twice.

Use a **partner-first beneficiary order independent of non-pension wealth**. If a modelled partner survives, they receive the whole remaining unaccessed pot. Otherwise, divide the pot equally among identified living child branches; a reliably linked descendant can represent a child who died earlier. If neither a partner nor a recorded child branch is available, use the two-recipient synthetic process described above and label those receipts separately. Same-year deaths leave neither adult eligible as a surviving partner. A known but unresolved child branch receives one matched synthetic stand-in rather than increasing the linked children's shares. The earlier statutory-legacy percentage formula is preserved only in the [historical first-death design](wealth-inheritance-first-death-alternative.md).

This is a modelling convention, not an automatic legal transfer to all family members. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/tax-on-pension-death-benefits) says the member usually nominates beneficiaries and a provider can sometimes pay someone else. [MoneyHelper](https://www.moneyhelper.org.uk/en/pensions-and-retirement/pension-problems/pensions-after-death) distinguishes passable defined-contribution pots from defined-benefit pensions, which generally have no pot to pass on, and from annuities, which usually stop unless a protection feature was chosen. The WAS starting total includes source DB rights, capitalised pensions already in payment and former-partner rights. Treating all of it as a pot for a non-retired simulated person is a simplifying assumption, **not** a measurement of actually transferable pension funds. The source-component preparation scripts remain available to assess that approximation, but the modelled pot does not require a rebuilt input. SimPaths currently has no separate post-retirement stock representing the capital value of pension income. The model does not represent nominations, scheme rules, annuity survivor protection, or death-benefit taxes. The [2027 Inheritance Tax change](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inheritance-tax-unused-pension-funds-and-death-benefits) is also outside this simplified simulation; do not describe modelled pension lump sums as after-tax receipts.

When a childless person leaves both a transferable pot and the final pooled non-pension estate, the two synthetic recipients are the **same people** for both transfers. The payment ledger keeps pension and non-pension amounts separate. Net housing equity and the net financial balance are combined into one non-pension estate before that estate is split; they do not each trigger a new recipient draw.

## Implementation relationship and acceptance checks

The implementation reuses the annual settlement point, repaired parent IDs, child index, recipient balance updates, minor trusts, and auditable transfer output. Estate settlement and its supporting family-link and wealth accounting run in every wealth-module simulation. There is no `enableInheritance` flag or `bequestMode` selector: this pooled last-adult rule is the only implemented non-pension method. The scenario YAML files set `bequestSyntheticRecipientCount: 2` and `transferDcPensionAtDeath: true`. Each simulation writes `inheritance_transfers.csv`, `inheritance_death_events.csv`, `inheritance_balances.csv`, `inheritance_family_links.csv`, `inheritance_synthetic_matches.csv`, `alignment_wealth_flows.csv`, and `inheritance_method.txt` in its CSV output folder. The method file explicitly records that natural and alignment deaths trigger bequests while emigration does not, and whether pension transfers were enabled. The appended `death_cause` column in the transfer ledger distinguishes `Death` from `DeathAlignment`; trust releases without a deceased donor use `not_applicable`. The death-event ledger records every newly settled death, including deaths without a positive estate or transferable pot. A pooled estate with two same-year deaths is attributed to its settlement donor in the transfer ledger; use the death-event ledger to identify both partners' causes rather than counting payments as deaths.

The previous 2021–2022 smoke run at `output/20261001223145_606_0` excluded all 152 alignment deaths from bequests. It documents the superseded convention and must not be used to validate the revised rule. The dedicated [`inheritance_alignment_deaths_smoke.yml`](../config/inheritance_alignment_deaths_smoke.yml) enables pension transfers for the revised short check. Regression tests cover both death causes, first and final partner deaths, a mixed natural/alignment same-year death, descendant representation, pension retirement and switch-off, synthetic recipients, repeat settlement, emigration and exported death causes. Full scenario results and presentation figures still require new runs.

The configuration requested 5,000 people; the existing initial-population sampler produced 3,893. These are **unscaled simulated-sample amounts in 2015-price pounds**, not UK totals.

Payment counts differ from death counts: a first death may leave pooled wealth with a partner, an estate may generate multiple payments, and some deaths have no distributable wealth. All death-event causes matched the `Person.csv` exits; all immediate cash recipients were living at settlement. The alignment-flow ledger contained 253 unit entries, 147 emigrant-person pension rows and 99 whole-unit emigrations, with no death-estate externalisation rows. The 23 targeted inheritance and pension tests passed. The reproducible [death-cause checker](diagnostics/check_inheritance_death_causes.py) can be run on either full scenario's CSV folder after the reruns. These checks establish the revised trigger and export behaviour; they do not certify every estate's full source-to-recipient stock reconciliation or determine the new ageing results.

Before applying this rule to the ageing experiment, verify: a first death with surviving partner; both partners dying in one year; a surviving partner re-partnering while first partner's children retain eligibility; no children; partly missing child links; same-benefit-unit and cross-unit children; minors; home equity and mortgage debt; unsecured debt and insolvent units; a small or empty matching cell; deterministic recipient selection; pension transfer at individual death; conservation of estate value; and correct personal and benefit-unit balances after a move. Audit identified-child, synthetic-recipient, unmatched, and written-off amounts by year and demographic scenario. Then rerun both population scenarios with the **same bequest parameters**; earlier outputs are not comparable as results of this design.
